## 3. Ground rules (non-negotiable)

From CLAUDE.md and REDESIGN.md, the ones that matter most tonight:

- **The server owns all state.** Remotes carry intents only, and every handler validates range, ownership and rate (RateLimiter). A new remote needs a name in `Net.RemoteNames` and an entry in `RateLimiter` LIMITS, or its requests are silently dropped.
- **Only EconomyService changes cash** (`EconomyService.AddCash`, clamped at `GameConfig.CashCap`). Only ProfileService touches DataStores. New save fields go in `ProfileSchema` (type + template, plus `Migrate` if old saves need converting). Never rename or remove profile fields.
- **Never edit** `src/ServerScriptService/Vendor/`.
- **No balance changes tonight.** Leave the values in WoodData, ItemCatalog and BiomeData alone, and keep the tree counts as they are. The standing rule is: after any balance change, run `lune run tools/economy` and commit ECONOMY.md. Tonight, if `lune run tools/economy --check` fails after your change, you changed balance by accident, so back it out. The model reads WoodData, ItemCatalog, BiomeData and a few GameConfig values, not WorldPlan, so it can't see a change to how many choppable trees there are or where: keep those by hand. Colour fields (tree palettes, truck paint) are fine.
- **Art modules are pure code** (`src/ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Art`): `Instance.new` and datatypes only, no services. Place parts with `.CFrame`, never `.Position`. Randomness comes from `Kit.rng(seed)`, never `math.random`. Decoration goes through `Kit.decor` (no collide, no query, no touch). Builders return a Model around the origin with the ground at y = 0 and the front facing -Z.
- **Game code is `--!strict`.** Put rules in pure modules with a spec, and add or update a spec with every rule change.
- **Keep the contracts** in REDESIGN.md ("Contracts other code relies on"): part names, tags and attributes that other code finds. Raise a part budget in its spec on purpose. Never bypass or delete a test.
- **Don't move** the town, the sawmill (0, 110), the sell zone (0, 86), the spawn (`town.spawn`, (0, 0.5, 40)), Murph's camp, or any biome centre or radius. The economy model and many specs measure from them.
- **Tutorial step ids are save-migration keys.** Never rename or reorder them. Editing their text is safe.
- **Claude can't run Studio.** Every change ships with exact Studio checks.
- **Land on `main`** (Connor asked for everything there), in small green merges. Commit messages: `phase-10: <ID> <what changed>`, e.g. `phase-10: TWN-03 spawn faces the sawmill`.
- Ask Connor before deviating from GAME_DESIGN §14's data values or architecture.

## 5. Where we are

**The game.** A cozy low-poly lumber tycoon. The core loop: chop trees with an axe, carry the logs, load them into your truck, drive to town, sell at the sawmill, then buy better axes, trucks, a plot and blueprints. Murph's 7-step tutorial (fell, pickup, load, sell, plant, loads, steel) teaches it. Daily goals, milestones, a Field Guide and nine townsfolk keep it going. A gondola climbs the Skyroot (a giant landmark tree) to the floating Aether Isles: Sky Bin, forge, Featherfall glide, crystal nodes. Weather and a 20-minute day run on a shared clock.

**The twist: the Living Forest** (GAME_DESIGN §16):
- Felled trees drop Heartseeds. Plant one in a stump and the tree regrows in 8 s with your name on a stake.
- You earn a 15% planter's share when anyone else fells a tree you planted.
- Each biome has a grove vitality from 0 to 100 (Thinning, Healthy, Thriving, Old Growth). Thriving groves grow more figured wood, and Old Growth wakes a co-op Elder tree.
- Hidden Grain: some trees carry figured wood with visible bark clues (Curly x1.5, Birdseye x2, Quilted x2.5, Stormgrain x3 from lightning, Burl x4, Starfall Burl x8). The sawmill reveals it when you sell.
- The Skyroot blooms when the groves average 75+.

**What's built.** All of the above, on `main`. Every model is Luau code in `src/ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Art`. The world is generated: `TerrainGen` paints the biomes, `WorldPlan` places the town, 322 ground trees, boulders, signs and the gondola, and `MapBuilder` builds it all at server start. That is about 18,100 parts, 80% of them trees. The biomes are a starter forest, autumn hills, snowfields, a volcano, the Phantom Grove (night only), a palm coast with a river and lake, and the Skyroot with its isles. Two review rounds fixed 51 findings: ghost stumps, solo refell seeds, HUD popups, the build bar, gondola cable clearance, the truck recall confirm, left-behind logs saved across sessions, a Studio-only DataStore (`PlayerProfiles_Studio`), purchase retry, banked overflow cash, saved settings.

**Verified.** `./scripts/check.sh` is green: StyLua, Selene, luau-lsp strict types, 408 Lune tests, ECONOMY.md current. CI runs the same script. `rojo build` exits 0. The boot order and remote names were traced by hand and found consistent.

**Never verified.** Nothing has run in Roblox Studio or on a phone. Unknown: physics feel, streaming, frame rate, network ownership of trucks and logs, touch and gamepad input, and how materials and lighting look under Roblox's renderer. The previews are three.js: flat colours, no Roblox materials, a different bloom. Place settings may not match `default.project.json`, because `rojo serve` doesn't sync them.

**Per area, today:**
- **World.** The town and its view from the spawn work, and the Starter Forest and the Hills look finished. The land between places is empty: 78% of dry lowland 100-stud cells hold no tree or boulder, and the 1,200-stud haul to the Snowfields crosses flat lime lawn (about ±6 studs of relief). Each biome is a hard-edged disc. The snow is a low-contrast white blob. The volcano has flat orange paint-like patches. The waterfall is a static glass pole about 710 studs tall. The isle tops are bare. Unchecked runtime risks: `StreamingTargetRadius` 1024 probably drops the distant mountains, snow and volcano from the town's horizon; the first player waits 10-20 s with no character and no loading UI; the gondola is an anchored cabin moved by the server every Heartbeat, so it will likely stutter.
- **Town.** Each building is good and they share one construction style. The real town, though, is a few buildings on a bare 265x237-stud field: no trees, and the nearest tree is 87 studs from the spawn, even though Murph's first line is "See that oak?". The preview scene `town.luau` is a stale hand copy with 22 trees and 30 bushes the game doesn't have. The spawn faces south, onto the parking lot. The lot is 24 cream slabs larger than the shop row, and it is unlit at night. On a phone you can't tell the shops apart from the spawn. The roads north start in a field behind the mill.
- **Trees and forest.** The strongest art: nine woods, each with its own silhouette and palette, and charming critters. The weak spots are the moments every new player sees. The HP bar floats 18-34 studs up, off-screen. Chips fall through the ground in 0.25 s. The fall dust uses the default sparkle texture. Stumps have fins sticking up past the cut. The tutorial sprout hangs in mid-air. Canopies probably hide the player, because there is no leaf fade. Frostwood blends into the snow, a Thriving grove looks like a Thinning one, and Elders don't read as giants.
- **Characters, vehicles, tools.** Distinct NPCs, trucks and axes, and well-built vehicle physics. The player's own character is the problem: carried logs float while the arms hang down; the chop plays in whatever direction you face; trucks turn about their middle, so the tail slides out; hats probably poke through the cab roofs; the box skid may stop dead at a ledge.

On phones, toasts cover the clock, the music button, the pouch, Daily Goals and the tutorial tracker, and the hint and toasts draw over shop panels. Nothing shows logs carried or bed fill. The hint never changes and nothing explains driving. Too much is on screen in minute one. Tap targets are 26-40 px and labels 11-13 px. The tutorial's plant step can dead-end.
- **Runtime.** The server is cheap (anchored logs, one massive part per truck, no shadowed lights). The client is the concern. About 9,700 parts stream in at the spawn. WindSway moves up to 2,304 shadow-casting parts a frame in the Snowfields. Nothing scales with device or quality setting. Chopping plays the jump-landing sound and trucks are silent. One error in MapBuilder means nobody ever spawns.

## 11. Gotchas learned the hard way

**Git and agents**
- Make agent worktrees yourself, from the game repo: `git -C "$REPO" worktree add "$REPO/../tt-wt/<stream>" -b fix/<stream> origin/main`. Then check `git -C <wt> remote get-url origin`. The harness's own worktree isolation once pointed at the wrong repo.
- Copy `globalTypes.d.luau` from `$REPO` into each new worktree (it is gitignored, and check.sh downloads it otherwise). `preview/` is per worktree and gitignored.
- Merge only after a workflow has finished. An agent once rewrote its commit after it had been merged. Agents never push to `main`; only the lead does.

**Tools**
- Read check.sh's real exit code. Piping it into `tail` hides failures.
- StyLua: run `stylua src tests tools`. Selene flags unused requires, so delete them.
- Luau strict: forward-declare locals used before their definition (`local f: (T) -> ()`, then `f = function ... end`). A pcall of a function that returns nothing needs `function(): any ... return nil end`. Mixed-type arrays need an explicit `{ any }`.
- Lune 0.10.5 gets `CFrame.lookAt` and `CFrame.new(pos, at)` wrong. `tools/loader.luau` shims them for loaded modules, but in Art and Shared code prefer `CFrame.fromMatrix` or `Angles` (as alongX/alongZ and NPCData.YawOf do). In Lune, read `part.CFrame.Position`; `part.Position` errors with "missing default value".
- Renders: `world` and `terrain` views take up to ~3 min each. render.mjs waits 120 s for the scene and 180 s per screenshot. Shoot one view per command. The viewer draws only the first 24 point lights.

**Game contracts that break silently**
- MapBuilder: CharacterAutoLoads stays false until the end, SellArea is parented last (GameServer starts when it appears), and clients wait on the `TimberlineMap` folder name.
- Persistent models: SellArea (SellZone, SellPoint, SkyBin), the shops' Counter/ShopPoint, Murph's Torso and the gondola stations are waited on with no timeout. Don't rename them or make them Atomic.
- Names and tags clients find:
  - parts named WindowGlass, LampGlow and LanternGlow;
  - tags StreetLamp, SawBlade, Flame, CloudChute, StarfallForge, ParkingSlot (+Index), Tree, Node (crystal nodes), Stump, Log, Truck, NPC, PlacedItem (plot builds);
  - part names Waterfall, WaterfallMist, WaterfallSpray;
  - canopy part names Leaves, Frond, Tier, Facet (WindSway, CanopyFade);
  - QuestUI.BeaconPaths (SellArea/SellZone, ToolShed/Counter).
- SawBlade teeth stay welded and unanchored. Never re-anchor them.
- The SellPad stays non-colliding and at most 0.25 tall. Its `ANG(0, PI, 0)` is what makes the text read upright.
- ParkingSlot parts: VehicleService reads their CFrame, Size.Y, tag and Index. They are 10 x 60 (WorldPlan.ParkingSlotSize, M1.5), one row of 12. Restyle them, but keep that contract.
- Leaves stay CanCollide false (Poppercam and trucks), and canopies stay CanQuery true (clicking leaves targets the tree).
- Isle tops stay flat, colliding discs. New isle decor is non-colliding and honours `opts.clear`.
- Gondola cable constants (GONDOLA_OUT, GONDOLA_RIM, CABLE_UP, CABLE_SIDE, CABLE_CLEAR) were just fixed and are covered by specs.
- TruckArt: the Chassis is the only massive part besides the skids. The skids are frictionless with bottoms at y 0. DriverSeat is a direct child with CanCollide off. Only beds, walls and skids collide. Every rig (truck + trailer) fits a 10 x 60 slot (M1.5; was 6.5 x 37).
- AxeArt: Handle is the PrimaryPart, everything else is welded to it, and `AxeArt.Grip` is verified.
- EnvironmentData's local `checkOverride` asserts that every override key exists. A typo errors at require time and kills the client's lighting.
- DecorSpots must draw the same rng numbers per candidate, or every decor cell reshuffles.
- Model:ScaleTo on the client (TreeFX.regrow, ForestUI.onSprout) can catch server-added BarkClue/StormGlow parts mid-animation and mis-scale them. Check in Studio by planting a seed that rolls a figure.
- GameConfig.SwingCooldown is read by both ChopController and GameServer's swing check (and by the economy model), so one edit changes the client, the server and ECONOMY.md.
- Screen DisplayOrders are a set: FigureReveal 12, TimberlineHUD 10, SettingsUI 10, BlueprintPlacer 6, ShopUI 5, DailyUI 3, HeartseedPouch 3, Corner 2, PlotUI 2, SideButtons 1, LeftColumn 0. Change them together. SideButtons, ShopUI and FigureReveal are IgnoreGuiInset on purpose, so SideButtons' offset conversions and the HudLayout tests move together.
- `UITheme.IsPhone()` needs touch, no keyboard and a viewport under 600 tall, so an iPad gets desktop sizes. Test both paths.
- `Kit.signText` (PixelsPerStud 40, LightInfluence, font) changes every sign in the game, the isles included.

**The engine draws terrain where it likes (learned on the live game, October 2026).** The engine drew the flat town 2 studs (half a voxel) above the y = 0 TerrainBuilder wrote: NPCs waist-deep, parked trucks, the tent and the campfire buried, the lot paint gone, spawns underground. Test pads far below the map (TER-01) didn't catch it, so TerrainBuilder now writes the town's chunks first and shifts them until rays read the town at 0 (TER-04, `Shared/World/TerrainFit.NextShift`), then writes the whole world with that shift. Output prints `[TerrainBuilder] Town ground: written at 0, drawn at ...; heights shifted ...` and `Town ground at y = ...`; Workspace gets TerrainShift and TownGroundY. `TerrainBuilder.LiftOnto` is the second guard for NPCs, parked trucks and the spawn. Keep placing things at HeightAt. Grass decoration grows through thin parts on Grass terrain: put a non-grass TownFloors patch under any new building (TER-02).
- **Collisions (COL-01).** `Kit.solid` undoes `Kit.decor`; `Kit.rock` collides; scenery keeps its builders' collision; `BuildingArt.Signpost(text, lit?, solidBoard?)`; sky bridges have invisible `BridgeGuard` parts (Roblox ignores CanQuery = false while CanCollide is true, so rays can hit them). Client-only decor (AmbientLife) must never collide: a collider only one client has fights the driver-simulated truck physics.

**Prebuilt terrain.** If the published place has the `Workspace.Terrain` attribute `Prebuilt = true`, TerrainBuilder skips the build. Any later TerrainGen, WorldLayout or BiomeData change then leaves trees and signs floating or buried until the terrain is rebuilt. Prebake last. Publishing a fresh `build.rbxlx` over the place afterwards throws the baked terrain away (and its attribute), which is safe but brings back the slow start.
