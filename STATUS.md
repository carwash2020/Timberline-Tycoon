### How plots work

14 pre-built homestead pads (`PlotData.Homes`), one per player slot plus two spares. A player buys one at the Land Office (`PlotService.Claim`, range-checked, charged once, refunded if the build fails). It starts as an 80-stud Campsite and grows through tiers 80/110/140/170/200 ($2,500 / $10,000 / $30,000 / $60,000), plus individually bought 40-stud squares out to a 5x5 grid (square k costs `ExpansionPriceStep` 3050 x k; the first costs `FirstPlotPrice` 100). Each pad stands on a raised dark grey platform (`Shared/PlotPlatform`, 1 stud, ramps all round, grows with the tier and the bought squares); plot-local y = 0 is its top. Builds are saved in plot-local studs so a layout loads on any pad. Limits: 200 prebuilt pieces and 400 kit pieces; selling returns half. A base can be unloaded from the SAVES panel (saved with its save, pad freed) and loaded back onto any pad (`BaseLogic`); saves are picked by hand at join and only a save with a plot opens the pad picker. Rules live in `PlotData` and `PlotLogic`, the server side in `PlotService`. Tier and square prices are a first pass and are not tuned in the economy model yet.

## Tooling notes

- Toolchain via Rokit (`bash scripts/cloud-setup.sh` on Linux). `lune run tests/run [Name]` runs one spec. `bash tools/preview/shoot.sh <scene>` renders a scene to `preview/` for art or world changes.
- `./scripts/check.sh` takes several minutes (WorldPlan.spec alone is about a minute); run it in the background or with a long timeout, since foreground commands time out near 600 s.
- Do not use `pkill -f` patterns that match your own shell.
